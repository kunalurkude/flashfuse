How EV range is calculated

Every battery stores energy, measured in watt-hours (Wh). Every vehicle uses a certain amount of that energy for each kilometre. Range is just one divided by the other:

Range (km) = Voltage × Ah × usable share ÷ Wh per kmUsable share: about 90% for lithium-ion, 95% for LFP, 50% for lead-acid (to protect its life).

The battery label gives you voltage and Ah. The hard part is the Wh per km, because it depends on the vehicle, the load, your speed and the roads. EV Studio uses figures from real-world tests and owner reports instead of brochure claims.

Worked examples

60V 50Ah lithium scooter

60 × 50 = 3,000 Wh. Usable: 3,000 × 0.9 = 2,700 Wh. Range: 2,700 ÷ 33 ≈ 82 km on a typical day, roughly 54–108 km depending on load and riding.

48V 100Ah lead-acid e-rickshaw

48 × 100 = 4,800 Wh, but only about half is used per cycle: 2,400 Wh. Range: 2,400 ÷ 31 ≈ 77 km.

30 kWh electric car

30,000 × 0.9 = 27,000 Wh. Range: 27,000 ÷ 128 ≈ 211 km in real-world mixed driving.

How long charging takes

Lithium batteries charge at full current until about 85–95% full, then the charger slows down to top them off safely. For a rough estimate, divide the battery's Ah by your charger's amps, then add a little for the slow finish. A 50Ah pack on a 10A charger reaches about 95% in 5 hours and full in about 5.5. Lead-acid batteries take longer: around Ah × 1.2 ÷ amps to reach 90%, then about 3 more hours to finish.

Common questions

Why is my real range lower than the company's claim?

Company figures come from slow, gentle test cycles. Real roads add speed, weight, hills, traffic and heat or cold. The calculator gives a range band rather than a single number for this reason.

Is 1 unit of electricity the same as 1 kWh?

Yes. Your electricity bill counts in units, and one unit is one kilowatt-hour.

Should I use lithium or lead-acid for an e-rickshaw?

Lead-acid is cheaper upfront, but you can only use about half its capacity and it wastes more energy while charging. Lithium (especially LFP) gives more range from the same rated capacity and lasts many more cycles.

Does the calculator work for electric cars?

Yes. For cars you enter the battery in kWh and the charger in kW instead of volts and amps.
